About

Systems that keep working after the demo ends.

I'm a robotics engineer with two years of industry experience. My work sits where the hard parts of robotics meet: the sensor data is noisy, the actuators are cheap, and the robot still has to walk, look, and respond in real time.

Most recently I've been building the locomotion and state-estimation stack for a twelve-motor quadruped, from a physics model and closed-form inverse kinematics through an omnidirectional gait, a coupled-oscillator closed loop, contact detection, and leg odometry, all validated in MuJoCo and ported to Isaac Sim before it touches hardware.

On the perception side I write real-time vision pipelines in Rust that run on edge hardware: stereo depth, detection, face identification, and servo tracking in one loop. I care about system identification over guesswork, about firmware that degrades gracefully, and about measuring a thing before claiming it works.

01industry · private

Quadruped locomotion in simulation

Built the full locomotion stack for a small four-legged robot with linkage legs driven by twelve Dynamixel servos. Started from the CAD, ended with a robot that walks in any direction.

  • MuJoCo model with closed kinematic loops, masses measured on hardware, centre of mass verified against the real robot.
  • Closed-form per-leg inverse kinematics with a self-test against the simulator.
  • Statically stable forward walk, then an omnidirectional gait taking a (vx, vy, ω) command. Stable in all eight commanded directions.
  • Four coupled phase oscillators as the gait clock, with contact reflexes and posture, balance and speed loops closing around them.
  • Ablation studies on the passive ankle spring, drop tests, and a parameter sweep harness.

A Rust pipeline that takes a side-by-side stereo frame, runs person detection, tracks people across frames, identifies faces against an authorised set, reads emotion, computes depth from disparity, and drives yaw and pitch servos to hold the target. One loop, edge hardware.
